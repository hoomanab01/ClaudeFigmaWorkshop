Your design system is the prompt.

Naming Is Functional

A layer name used to be a note to your teammate. It is now an instruction.

What you shipped
✕Frame 427
✕Rectangle 12 copy
✕Result: a component no one can name, referenced nowhere, rebuilt every time it appears.
What it needed
✓Badge/Status/Open
✓Card/Metric/Positive
✓Result: one component, four variants, referenced by name everywhere it appears.
Rename before you prompt. It is the cheapest thing you will do tonight.
Concrete, and slightly embarrassing. Ask them to open any recent client file and read three layer names aloud in their head. Nobody volunteers. That is the point. This is the moment to say renaming is not admin, it is the spec.

The Rules File

One file the model reads on every single run, so you stop repeating yourself in chat.

Layer 01
Sources of truth
Where the tokens live. Which components are canonical. Which frame is the reference for this screen.
Layer 02
Constraints
Spacing comes from the scale. Type comes from the five steps. Every interactive element ships with its states.
Layer 03
Never
No new colors. No hardcoded values. No one-off overrides to make a single screen look right.
Show the actual file for ten seconds if the room is technical enough, otherwise stay on the slide. The third card is the one they underestimate: without a "never" list the model is helpful in exactly the wrong way. Tell them the template is in the take-home pack.

Chapter 2
The Build
35 minutes, hands on keyboards. Vidushi takes tooling failures, I take design decisions. Announce the split out loud so people know who to flag. Target: everyone clears rung 3 by 18:50, rung 4 is the last ten minutes.

The Ladder

Four rungs. Each one proves something the one before it could not.

  1. 01One componentBuild Button/Primary with its variants. Proves the token path is real and not a coincidence.
  2. 02One blockBuild the metric row from the component you just made. Proves reuse, and catches the model quietly cloning instead of referencing.
  3. 03The screen, with statesEmpty, loading, error. Proves the part nobody gets for free, because nobody asks for it.
  4. 04The change requestOne token moves. Proves whether you built a system or a very tidy screenshot.
Read rung 4 last and slowly. That is the rung the evening is built around. Everything before it is setup for that moment. If the room is running late, cut rung 2, never rung 4.

Change one token. Watch the whole screen follow.

Rung four
The payoff moment of the evening. Do it on the projector first, live, in one edit. Then send them to do it. If the screen follows, the system held. If they have to touch five files, the system leaked and that is the real lesson. Either result is a win, say so before they start.
Hands onChapter 2 · The Build

The Change Request

1Open the Change Request frame. Find the two things that moved.
2Change the tokens. Change nothing else. Do not let the model "help" by editing components.
3If you had to touch a component to get there, your system leaked. Find exactly where.
Step 3 is the teaching, not steps 1 and 2. Collect two or three leak locations from the room, you will reuse them in the critique chapter. Hard stop at 19:00 whatever state they are in.
Chapter 3
The Critique
My hour. Twenty minutes teaching the method, thirty minutes of them running it on their own build, ten minutes of showing two or three on the projector. This is the part that makes the evening a design workshop rather than a tools class.

Plausible, Not Correct

The model is extremely good at producing something that looks finished.

What you get for free
✓A layout that reads at a glance
✓Colors that are roughly the right colors
✓Copy sitting in the right places
✓Something you could screenshot and post
What you never get unless you ask
✕Spacing that holds the scale instead of drifting
✕Hierarchy that survives real content lengths
✕The states nobody drew
✕A visible focus ring and a usable target size
The right column is your job. It is also the part that is not automated.
Do not let this sound anti-AI. The left column is genuinely valuable and took a week five years ago. The argument is about where the designer's scarcity moved, not about whether the tool is good. Watch the tone here, the room contains both evangelists and sceptics.

Six Passes

Run them in this order. Structure before surface, always, or you fix the same thing twice.

  1. 01TokensIs every value coming from the system, or has something been hardcoded?
  2. 02StructureAre these your named components, or lookalikes rebuilt from scratch?
  3. 03HierarchyDoes the eye land where the design sent it, with real content in place?
  4. 04StatesHover, focus, disabled, empty, loading, error. All six, every time.
  5. 05ResponsiveWhat breaks at 390 pixels wide, and what silently overflows?
  6. 06Accessibility floorContrast, visible focus, target size, and semantics that match the meaning.
The reference slide of the evening. Leave it up during their critique block. Justify the order with one example: fixing spacing on a component that should not exist wastes the fix twice. Tell them this checklist is in the take-home pack, so nobody photographs it.

The Critique Prompt

Hand the model your checklist and make it audit its own work against the frame.

Compare the current build against the frame Inbox/Desktop. Run these six passes, in order: tokens · structure · hierarchy · states · responsive · accessibility For each pass, list what fails and the exact file and line. Do not fix anything yet. I will decide what gets fixed.
Separate finding from fixing. The finding can be delegated. The decision cannot.
The last line is the whole slide. If you let it fix while it finds, you lose the review and inherit changes you never read. Demo the difference live if there is time: same build, with and without the final line. This prompt ships in the take-home pack.

The Two Traps

We planted two faults in the starter file. Your build inherited both, silently.

Trap one
✕A component named Group 12
✕It compiled. It rendered. It looks correct on screen, and nobody on your team can reference it, extend it or find it again.
Trap two
✕One card using #2E7D32 instead of a token
✕It survived rung four untouched. Every other card followed the change. That one card is now permanently wrong.

Neither of these is a model failure. Both are handoff failures, and both were yours to catch.

The best five minutes of the night if you time it right. Do not reveal the traps until at least one person has found one on their own, then credit them by name. If nobody found them, that is a stronger point, not a weaker one.

Monday Morning

How to point this at your own work without blocking a week on it.

  1. 01Pick a screen you already designedNot a new idea. Something shipped, something real, something you already know is right.
  2. 02Rename it properly and write the rules fileOne hour, no code. This is the part everyone skips and the part that decides the result.
  3. 03Build one component, then ask for the change requestIf the change propagates, your system is real. If it does not, you found the leak in an hour instead of a quarter.
If step two feels slow, that is the work. It is also the return.
Be specific about the hour. Designers will not start a system rewrite, but they will spend one hour renaming one screen. That is the wedge. Offer the room a follow-up: send me what you built in a week.

Fix the system, not the screen.
